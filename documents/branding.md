# ZakTec Solutions Brand Guidelines

## Purpose of this document

This guide defines how ZakTec Solutions should look, sound and be presented across the website, web applications, social media and marketing materials.

It does not describe the application architecture or development workflow; those belong in `technical-ai-document.md`. It does not explain how visitors use the website; that belongs in `human-friendly-web-app-guide.md`.

## Brand name

The correct public name is:

**ZakTec Solutions**

Always preserve this exact capitalisation and spacing.

Do not use:

- ZAKTec Solutions
- ZAKTEC Solutions
- Zaktec Solutions
- Zak Tech Solutions
- ZakTecSolutions

Machine identifiers remain lowercase where required:

- Domain: `zaktecsolutions.com`
- Email: `hello@zaktecsolutions.com`
- Repository and package name: `zaktecsolutions`

## Brand purpose

ZakTec Solutions exists to help businesses and organisations use AI and digital technology in a clear, manageable and responsible way.

The company focuses on real organisational needs rather than promoting technology for its own sake.

## Positioning

ZakTec Solutions is a UK AI and digital consultancy providing:

- AI consultancy and automation
- Digital systems
- Websites and online services
- Related training and support

The company should be presented as a focused professional consultancy. It should not appear to be:

- Only a website-design company
- Only an education technology company
- A generic AI agency
- A large established consultancy
- A government contractor unless evidence is confirmed
- A founder-focused personal portfolio

## Target audience

Primary audiences are:

- Small and medium-sized businesses
- Charities and community organisations
- Education and training providers
- Professional service businesses
- Public-sector organisations

Secondary audiences include independent specialists and delivery partners interested in collaborating.

Referring to an audience does not mean that the audience is an existing client. Copy must not imply previous contracts or results without evidence.

## Brand personality

ZakTec Solutions should feel:

- Professional
- Practical
- Calm
- Clear
- Responsible
- Approachable
- Credible
- User-focused

The brand should not feel:

- Loud
- Overconfident
- Corporate for its own sake
- Experimental with client needs
- Overly technical
- Trend-led
- Impersonal

## Tone of voice

Use concise, professional British English.

Write at the reader’s level:

- Start with their need or problem.
- Explain technology in plain language.
- Use short paragraphs and useful headings.
- State limitations where they matter.
- Give a clear next step.
- Sound confident without exaggeration.

Prefer active, direct wording:

> We review the current process before recommending a solution.

Avoid vague passive wording:

> A range of transformative solutions can be leveraged.

## Key messages

Use these ideas consistently. They do not need to appear word-for-word on every page.

### Primary message

> ZakTec Solutions helps businesses and organisations use technology more effectively through AI consultancy, automation, digital systems and professional websites.

### Supporting messages

- Start with the organisational need, not the technology.
- AI should solve a real problem.
- Digital systems should fit the people and processes that use them.
- Websites should explain services clearly and help visitors take the next step.
- Scope, risks, responsibilities and delivery requirements should be transparent.
- Training and support can help staff use new tools responsibly.

## Service naming

Use these main public service names:

1. **AI Consultancy and Automation**
2. **Digital Systems**
3. **Websites and Online Services**

Use **Training and Support** as related work within the wider consultancy offer rather than as a fourth homepage service.

Short navigation labels may be used where space is limited:

- AI Consultancy
- Digital Systems
- Websites

## Logo and wordmark

### Current approved treatment

The active website uses a text wordmark:

**ZakTec Solutions**

It appears in white inside a simple purple border on a dark background. The header uses a compact version and the footer uses a larger version.

The favicon uses a compact `ZT` graphic because the full name is not readable at browser-tab size.

### Usage rules

- Preserve the exact name and capitalisation.
- Keep the wordmark horizontal.
- Use it on a dark, high-contrast background.
- Keep sufficient space around the border so it does not touch nearby text or page edges.
- Link the website wordmark to the homepage.
- Use the accessible label `ZakTec Solutions home` for the homepage link.
- Keep the wordmark sharp and undistorted.

### Do not

- Stretch or compress the wordmark.
- Rotate it.
- add shadows, bevels or strong glow effects.
- Change the border to an unrelated colour.
- Place it over a busy image.
- Use the favicon as a full replacement for the wordmark in prominent brand positions.
- Create an unapproved symbol or strapline.

## Colour palette

The palette is dark, restrained and purple-led.

### Primary colours

| Use | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Main background | `hsl(228 35% 7%)` |
| Main foreground | `hsl(220 24% 96%)` |
| Primary purple | `hsl(265 82% 66%)` |
| Primary button text | `hsl(0 0% 100%)` |

### Supporting colours

| Use | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Card background | `hsl(226 30% 11%)` |
| Secondary surface | `hsl(226 24% 16%)` |
| Supporting text | `hsl(218 15% 70%)` |
| Border | `hsl(226 22% 22%)` |
| Header background | `#0b0d18` |
| Footer background | `#090b14` |

### Colour rules

- Use purple for primary actions, focus, small highlights and selected status accents.
- Keep large page areas dark navy rather than bright purple.
- Use white for primary headings.
- Use light slate for supporting text.
- Ensure text and controls maintain suitable contrast.
- Do not use colour alone to communicate status or meaning.
- Avoid adding unrelated bright colours without a clear functional reason.

## Typography

The website uses `Inter var` where available, followed by the system sans-serif stack.

### Hierarchy

- H1: strong, concise page message
- H2: clear section heading
- H3: card or subsection heading
- Body: plain explanatory text
- Eyebrow: short uppercase section label with increased letter spacing

### Typography rules

- Keep one H1 per page.
- Use sentence case for headings.
- Keep headings short enough to scan.
- Use body text of approximately `1rem` or larger.
- Keep important card text around `0.95rem` or larger.
- Use comfortable line spacing.
- Keep paragraph widths readable.
- Avoid long blocks of capital letters.
- Do not use decorative typefaces that weaken clarity.

## Buttons and interface styles

### Primary button

Use for the main action on a page or section.

- Solid purple background
- White semibold text
- Rounded corners
- Minimum 48-pixel height
- Clear hover and keyboard-focus states

Example:

> Discuss Your Project

### Secondary button

Use for a supporting action.

- Dark or transparent surface
- Light border
- White text
- Purple border or surface change on hover

Example:

> Explore Our Services

### Text link

Use for lower-priority navigation within content.

- Purple text
- Descriptive wording
- Underline on hover
- Visible focus state

### Cards and form controls

- Use dark surfaces with subtle light borders.
- Keep corner radius and padding consistent.
- Avoid placing excessive copy in a card.
- Keep form labels visible above fields.
- Use touch targets at least 44 pixels high.
- Keep status badges readable in text, such as **In development**.

## Image and icon style

The current website does not depend on stock photography.

When imagery is added:

- Prefer genuine project screens, interface details or relevant working context.
- Obtain approval before publishing project screenshots.
- Use calm, purposeful compositions.
- Keep colour treatment compatible with the dark and purple palette.
- Add useful alternative text when an image communicates information.
- Use empty alternative text for purely decorative images.

Icons should be:

- Simple
- Consistent in stroke and visual weight
- Easy to recognise
- Used to support text rather than replace it

Avoid:

- Generic futuristic AI imagery
- Glowing brains, robots or circuit-board clichés
- Unrelated stock-office photographs
- Decorative icons with no clear meaning
- Mixing several unrelated icon styles

## Writing rules

### Use

- British English spelling
- Short paragraphs
- Specific service and problem descriptions
- Descriptive calls to action
- Honest project status labels
- Clear statements of limitations
- “Businesses and organisations”
- “Public-sector organisations”
- “AI consultancy”
- “Digital systems”
- “Responsible AI”
- “Human oversight”
- “Discuss Your Project”

### Use carefully

These words are acceptable but should not be repeated in every section:

- Practical
- Improve
- Digital
- Organisation
- Solution
- Responsible

### Avoid

- Revolutionary
- Cutting-edge
- Unlock your potential
- Transform your business overnight
- Seamless solutions
- Your trusted partner
- Guaranteed results
- Bespoke excellence
- World-leading
- Industry-leading
- Game-changing

Also avoid unexplained technical terms such as:

- API
- LLM
- Vector database
- Headless architecture
- Serverless
- RAG

Use a plain explanation when a technical term is necessary.

## Claims and evidence

Do not invent or imply:

- Clients
- Government contracts
- Testimonials
- Statistics
- Measured results
- Qualifications
- Certifications
- Accreditations
- Partnerships
- Team members
- Prices

Do not describe a developing or demonstration project as a finished client system.

Do not describe frontend-only behaviour as secure. Security claims require evidence about the complete service.

## Social media and marketing

- Use the same company name, service names and core messages as the website.
- Keep posts informative and concise.
- Explain the problem, the useful idea and the next step.
- Distinguish company work from commentary or educational content.
- Do not imply a project is a client engagement unless confirmed.
- Do not publish screenshots, names or results without approval.
- Use the purple accent consistently without turning every asset into a bright gradient.
- Link to the most relevant existing page rather than using a generic homepage link for every post.

## Accessibility requirements

Brand consistency includes accessible presentation:

- Maintain sufficient colour contrast.
- Keep keyboard focus visible.
- Use readable text sizes and line spacing.
- Do not place essential text inside images.
- Provide alternative text for meaningful images.
- Keep headings in a logical order.
- Use descriptive link and button labels.
- Do not rely on colour alone.
- Keep controls large enough for touch use.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Test layouts at mobile, tablet and desktop widths.

## Consistency checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- The name is written as **ZakTec Solutions**.
- The service names match this guide.
- The copy uses British English.
- The message begins with a user or organisational need.
- Claims are supported and statuses are honest.
- The wordmark is clear and undistorted.
- Purple is used as an accent, not an overwhelming background.
- Typography and buttons match the established interface.
- Links and calls to action are descriptive.
- Contrast, focus and heading structure remain accessible.
